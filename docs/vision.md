# Product vision

Maceda is the artist’s electronic music alias. EQ-1 opens a family of studio tools that may grow to include compressors, preamps and saturators. A mix engineer should be able to find a resonance by ear, drag a narrow cut into place, type its exact frequency, A/B the result and reopen the session with the same sound.

## The screen in 1.0

The enlarged digital screen is the primary editor. Twelve independently addressable slots support bell, low shelf, high shelf, low pass and high pass. A logarithmic frequency axis, calibrated spectrum, combined response, draggable nodes and a selected-band inspector support both exploration and precise entry. Frequency determines node hue from low red to high purple. Numbers, selection outlines and enabled state carry meaning independently of color. The top frame holds red Maceda branding, presets, settings and global bypass. Pro-Q 4 informs the speed of editing. Use original layouts, graphics, typography and implementation.

The sound is linear, transparent and predictable. Dynamic EQ, spectral processing, EQ matching, analog coloration, modulation and linear phase are future product decisions. No hardware noise, saturation or component variation enters the signal path.

## The future instrument

A realistic fictional digital equalizer sits on a wooden recording-studio table. Its upper metallic-gray unit carries painted red Maceda lettering, a large animated EQ display and a small auxiliary status panel. Cables connect it to a lower controller on a black metallic stand. Each of its twelve panels carries a frequency knob, gain knob and translucent illuminated enable button colored by frequency. A separate power control engages global bypass.

Clicking the physical display enters the screen editor. Both views read the same slot IDs, parameter values, response and analysis data. A knob turn moves its node and a graph edit updates the knob and lamp. Screen-only controls expose Q and filter type. Retain stored gain on cut filters and visually indicate its inactive role. CMI V informs the transition between instrument and display, and Arturia effects inform the material treatment. The existing Blender prototype is a design reference for a later phase. Version 1.0 builds and runs without scene assets, rendering engines or Blender.
